Artificial intelligence is becoming a major force in healthcare, helping doctors diagnose disease, plan treatments, and monitor patients more effectively. Its strongest impact so far has been in medical imaging, where deep learning systems can detect issues like tumors, fractures, and eye diseases with accuracy comparable to skilled specialists.

AI is also speeding up drug discovery and improving predictive care. By analyzing huge datasets, it can identify promising drug candidates faster than traditional methods and help healthcare providers spot patients at risk of serious conditions or deterioration earlier, allowing more preventive and timely intervention.

At the same time, AI in healthcare raises important concerns around privacy, bias, transparency, regulation, and liability. The most promising path forward is to use AI as a support tool rather than a replacement for clinicians, combining machine speed and pattern recognition with human judgment, empathy, and ethical decision-making.
